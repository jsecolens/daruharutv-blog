You have a question about studying in Korea. You find a university email address, write a careful message, and wait. Then the reply arrives: "Please contact another office."

For an international student, finding the right person can feel like an extra assignment. A university may have several offices with similar English names, but each handles a different part of student life. Learning how to identify the responsible office can save you time, especially when a deadline is approaching.

---

## Start with the issue—and your student status

Before searching for a contact, describe your problem in one sentence. "I cannot register for a required course" is more useful than "I need academic help." Then identify your status: applicant, degree-seeking student, exchange student, visiting student, or graduate student.

This distinction matters. Check whether your university lists separate contacts for exchange students, visiting students, and international degree students. Use its contact directory to identify the team responsible for your student category instead of assuming that one international office handles everything.

The following table is a starting point, not a rule that applies to every university.

| Your question | A useful first place to check |
|---|---|
| An application you have not yet submitted | The admissions team responsible for your program |
| Exchange procedures or an exchange orientation | The exchange coordinator or international office listed in your documents |
| A required major course or a department-specific rule | Your department office or academic adviser |
| Registration, academic status, or certificates | The relevant academic affairs or student records office |
| A scholarship or student support program | The office named in that scholarship or program notice |
| A room assignment or dormitory maintenance problem | The housing office or residence management desk |
| A technical problem with the student portal | The university IT help desk |

Office names vary. Search for the function you need, not just a familiar English title.

---

## Read the notice before choosing an office

If your question concerns a particular announcement, look for its contact section first. Look for labels meaning "inquiries," "responsible office," or "contact details."

A scholarship announcement posted on an international office website may still direct questions to a different team. The contact listed for that specific announcement is usually a better starting point than a general university address.

Also check whether the notice belongs to your campus, college, program, and application round. Similar titles do not necessarily mean identical procedures.

---

## Make your first message easy to answer

Include the information needed to understand the problem:

- Your name, program, and student category.
- The course, notice, or service you are asking about.
- A link or the exact title of the relevant announcement.
- The deadline, if one applies.
- What you have already tried and one clear question.

If a screenshot helps, crop it to the relevant error. Use approved university channels for personal documents, and avoid attaching a passport or other sensitive information unless it is required through the appropriate process.

Here is a reusable email example:

> **Subject: Course registration question — [course code / program]**
> Hello, I am [name], a [student category] in [program]. I would like to register for [course], but the portal shows [error message]. I have checked [notice or guide]. Could you please confirm whether your office handles this issue, or direct me to the responsible team? The registration deadline is [date and time]. Thank you.

If you only need to confirm the contact, keep the question short:

> Could you confirm whether your office handles this inquiry? If another office is responsible, could you direct me to it?

---

## If you are redirected, keep the conversation connected

When another office takes over, forward the relevant explanation or summarize the previous response. This prevents you from having to describe the entire situation again.

For an approaching deadline, follow the notice's contact instructions and explain the urgency clearly. Do not assume that sending an inquiry pauses the deadline or counts as an application. A helpful reply and a completed submission are separate things.

Before ending the conversation, confirm your next action: which document, which system, and by what time. Keep the response until the issue is resolved.

You do not need to understand the university's entire organization chart. You need a reliable way to find the person responsible for the next step. That small skill can make campus life much easier.
